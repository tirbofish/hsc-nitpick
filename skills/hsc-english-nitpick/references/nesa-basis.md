# NESA evidence base

Use this reference to calibrate strictness. It distils official guidance; it does not reproduce student responses.

## What the official samples establish

NESA states that standards materials illustrate work typical at **band borderlines**. Do not describe Band 5/6 samples as confirmed 20/20 essays. The English Advanced collection includes a 2019 exemplar and Band 5/6 samples across Paper 1 Section I, the Common Module extended response, Module A, Module B, and Module C.

## Recurring features of stronger responses

Across recent NESA marking feedback, stronger responses repeatedly:

- engage with **all** parts and key terms of the particular question;
- sustain an informed, conceptual argument instead of reproducing a prepared response;
- make clear connections between evidence, textual choices, meaning, and the question;
- use detailed, well-chosen, accurate textual references;
- analyse form and language as meaning-making choices rather than list techniques;
- integrate contextual knowledge where relevant instead of attaching it as background;
- maintain control of structure, expression, purpose, form, and audience;
- in comparative work, balance and integrate both texts while evaluating how context, purpose, form, and language shape their relationship;
- in Craft of Writing, use the stimulus meaningfully, obey the required form, sustain a purposeful structure and voice, and explain the relationship between craft choices and intended meaning when reflection is required.

## Directive words

NESA warns against treating directive words as mechanical formulas: interpret them in the subject and question context. Its glossary defines the core actions used by this skill.

https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/hsc/student-guide/glossary

## Marking boundary

NESA marking guidelines combine syllabus outcomes with the knowledge and skills demanded by the specific question. Markers use the guidelines for that question; generic sophistication cannot compensate for missing a compulsory demand.

https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/hsc/exams-and-marking/marking-guideline-principles

## Calibration rule

When current question-specific guidelines or feedback are available, they outrank this synthesis. Use official NSW Government/NESA pages first. Do not treat commercial “20/20” essays as authoritative or copy their wording into feedback.
